Corpus Jr.** joined **Panel 2: The Quantum Countdown**.

## The Discussion

The conversation opened with the most basic question for a cybersecurity audience: **What is quantum?** Corpus answered first, and the moderator asked him to:

- explain quantum to a non-technical audience in plain language; and
- describe the difference between **quantum computing**, **quantum communications**, and **quantum sensing**, with a simple example of each.

The session closed with a question put to every panelist: **What does the quantum future look like?**

### Quantum in Brief

For readers new to the topic, the three areas in the opening question are distinct:

- **Quantum computing** uses qubits, which can exist in superpositions of states, to tackle certain problems, such as simulating molecules or breaking some of today's public-key cryptography, that are impractical for classical computers.
- **Quantum communications** uses quantum properties to secure the exchange of information. Quantum key distribution is the best-known example.
- **Quantum sensing** uses the sensitivity of quantum systems to measure quantities such as time, magnetic fields, and gravity with extraordinary precision.

## Why It Matters

Putting a quantum panel in front of a cybersecurity audience reflects a growing recognition that quantum computing is a security planning issue today, not only a research topic. Sufficiently powerful quantum computers could break widely used public-key cryptography, and encrypted data captured now could be decrypted later, so organizations are being urged to take stock of their cryptography and plan their migration early.

QCSP has been working on this with government, regulators, and industry. Earlier this year it convened the [Quantum Readiness Workshop at the Development Academy of the Philippines]({{site.baseurl}}/news/2026-04-24-quantum-readiness-workshop-dap/) and brought a [quantum readiness workshop to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas]({{site.baseurl}}/news/2026-08-28-bsp-quantum-readiness-workshop/).
