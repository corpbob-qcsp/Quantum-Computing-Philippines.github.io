<p class="text-muted text-center mb-4"><em>Professor of Physics &amp; Co-Director, Center for Undergraduate Research &middot; Saint Joseph's University</em></p>

<p>Dr. Roberto C. Ramos is a tenured full professor in the Department of Physics at Saint Joseph's University (SJU), where he also serves as Co-Director of the Center for Undergraduate Research and faculty advisor for the Society of Physics Students and Sigma Pi Sigma Physics Honor Society. His research expertise lies in quantum mechanics, ultra-low temperature physics, superconductivity, and quantum computing, with significant contributions to understanding quantum properties of materials and devices at millikelvin temperatures. He helped pioneer aspects of superconducting quantum computing using Josephson junction phase qubits.</p>

<p>In 2001, as part of the University of Maryland research group, Dr. Ramos co-authored the paper &ldquo;Design for Effective Thermalization of Junctions for Quantum Coherence&rdquo; (<em>IEEE Transactions on Applied Superconductivity</em>), which addressed critical challenges in preserving quantum coherence in current-biased Josephson junctions. This work was later cited by Nobel Laureate John M. Martinis in his influential review article &ldquo;Superconducting Phase Qubits&rdquo; (<em>Quantum Information Processing</em>, 2009), where Martinis referenced the Maryland group's proposal that qubits could be encoded in the ground and first excited states of a Josephson junction. Dr. Ramos' contribution forms part of the foundational scientific groundwork underlying modern superconducting qubit architectures.</p>

<p>His scholarly work spans basic and applied condensed matter physics, renewable energy, and physics education. Dr. Ramos has authored numerous publications in high-impact journals and secured substantial research and education grants from bodies including the National Science Foundation and private foundations.</p>

<p>An award-winning educator, he has received multiple honors such as the Christian R. and Mary F. Lindback Award for Distinguished Teaching (2023) and several Faculty Merit Awards for teaching, service, and diversity, equity, and inclusion. Beyond research and teaching, he is an active advocate for STEM outreach, notably founding the Physics Wonder Girls Program to encourage underrepresented students in science. Dr. Ramos earned his B.S. (cum laude) and M.S. in Physics from the University of the Philippines and his M.S./Ph.D. in Physics from the University of Washington.</p>
