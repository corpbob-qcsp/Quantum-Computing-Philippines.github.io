The **Theoretical Physics Spring School (TPSS)** is a four-month online course that introduces students to the fundamentals of theoretical physics. It is a new annual programme of **Quantum Wings**, to be piloted in 2027, and the **Quantum Computing Society of the Philippines (QCSP)** is one of its partner organizations.

Most of the mathematics needed to follow the topics is **not assumed as prior knowledge**. It is introduced and taught along the way, so students from different educational backgrounds can take part. The only prerequisite is an interest in theoretical physics.

<hr/>

### Why TPSS: The Philippines' PISA Results

The Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA) tests 15-year-olds in mathematics, reading, and science. In **PISA 2025**, whose results were released on 8 September 2026, Philippine scores were higher than in 2022 and 2018 in all three subjects. The gap to the rest of the world is still large, as the OECD's [country note for the Philippines](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/pisa-2025-results-volume-i-country-notes_2d4ff9ea-en/philippines_3699b9d5-en.html) shows:

| Share of students reaching at least Level 2 (minimum proficiency) | Philippines | OECD average |
|:--|:--:|:--:|
| Mathematics | 21% | 65% |
| Reading | 31% | 69% |
| Science | 32% | 74% |

Almost no Filipino students reached the top levels (Level 5 or 6) in mathematics, reading, or science. Level 2 in mathematics means being able to represent a simple situation mathematically without direct instructions, which is the starting point for the analytical skills that physics and engineering build on.

TPSS responds to these results by working on both interest and skill. It aims to **reignite students' interest in STEM** and strengthen their technical and mathematical skills through theoretical physics, by teaching the mathematics together with the physics rather than expecting students to arrive with it. For QCSP, this fits its wider work on quantum and STEM education, including its [collaboration with UP NISMED]({{site.baseurl}}/news/2026-10-01-up-nismed-quantum-basic-education/) on bringing quantum concepts into basic education.

### Programme

The 16 sessions are grouped into four modules. All of them are taught by Dr. Choong Pak Shen, with a standard text as the reference for each module.

| Module | Sessions (2027) | Reference text |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Classical physics I | 3, 10, 17, 24 January | V. I. Arnold, *Mathematical Methods of Classical Mechanics* |
| Statistical physics I | 31 January; 14, 21, 28 February | F. Reif, *Fundamentals of Statistical and Thermal Physics* |
| Relativity theory I | 7, 14, 21, 28 March | J. B. Hartle, *Gravity: An Introduction to Einstein's General Relativity* |
| Quantum physics I | 4, 11, 18, 25 April | A. Peres, *Quantum Theory: Concepts and Methods* |

There is no session on 7 February, which falls on the Chinese New Year weekend.

### Assessment and Certification

- **Examination:** a two-hour handwritten exam with four questions, held on **7 May 2027**. All Partnership Schools administer it at the same time so that conditions are consistent and fair.
- **Marking:** scripts are marked with the assistance of AI, and the Quantum Wings team reviews and verifies all results before they are released.
- **Grades:** relative grading. Grades A, B, C, and D correspond to the top 5%, the next 20%, the middle 50%, and the bottom 25% of participants.
- **Certificate of participation:** an electronic certificate for students who attend at least **14 of the 16 sessions**.
- **Certificate of distinction:** a printed certificate for students who take the examination through a Partnership School and rank in the **top 5%** of scorers.

Examination scripts, personal data, and individual results are used only for marking and assessment and are handled confidentially by authorised members of the Quantum Wings team. Only aggregated, anonymised statistics may be used for programme evaluation.

### About Quantum Wings

Quantum Wings is an international quantum education startup founded in Malaysia in December 2025. Its first programme, **QW Life: Finding your 45&deg;**, is a 90-minute session that introduces quantum science to students with no prior knowledge. It is built around a live demonstration with polarizing filters and the Polak game, and it invites students to think about their futures and the role of human agency in shaping them.

More than 700 students across Malaysia have taken part in the programme. It was also piloted in the Philippines during [QISTCon.PH 2026]({{site.baseurl}}/news/2026-11-11-qistcon-ph-2026/), where it reached 20 high school students.
